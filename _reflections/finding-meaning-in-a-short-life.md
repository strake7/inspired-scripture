Solomon said a live dog is better than a dead lion (Ecc. 9:4). The point is not
that the dog is noble. It is that while a creature still breathes it still has
hope, and the mightiest lion, once dead, has none.

Everything under the sun is **_he·ḇel_**, vapor that evaporates like smoke, and
Solomon faced the hardest fact about it: there is one fate for all. "There is
one fate for the righteous and for the wicked" (Ecc. 9:2). Abel was righteous,
and his name, _Havel_, is a near cousin of the very word Solomon used for
futility. His faith could not spare him a death at his brother's hand. No one
earns a way past the grave, "for all have sinned" (Rom. 3:23).

So how do you find meaning in a life you cannot lengthen? Solomon draws out
seven lessons, all of them pointing to Jesus, another name for Yahweh: (1)
faith, (2) hope, (3) grateful contentment, (4) service, (5) diligence, (6)
seeking His wisdom, and (7) trust.

## Hope only lasts as long as it rests in Jesus

Hope is free to every living person, but apart from Jesus it ends at the grave
(Ecc. 9:4-6). It was Christ who "brightened the dark valley," bringing life and
immortality to light. David knew where to anchor it while he still lived: "My
soul, wait in silence for God alone, for my hope is from Him" (Ps. 62:5).

## Whatever your hand finds to do, do it now

Death stalks like a predator, catching people "like fish caught in a treacherous
net" (Ecc. 9:12). You cannot control the length of your life, so do not delay.
"Whatever your hand finds to do, do it with all your might" (Ecc. 9:10), and do
it "as for the Lord and not for people" (Col. 3:23).

Grateful contentment with what God provides, quiet wisdom over the world's loud
counsel, and trust when foolish voices are popular round out Solomon's list. The
full study walks through all seven, and why each one only holds when it is
placed in Jesus.
